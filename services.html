Long-term Equity Portfolio Management

A research-led portfolio of quality Indian businesses, built to compound over years rather than trade over weeks. It is the core that other strategies can build on.

How it works

  1. 01

    Set the allocation

    How much of your capital belongs in equity is typically agreed from your goals, timeline and risk profile.

  2. 02

    Research and select

    Companies are screened on business quality, earnings growth, balance-sheet strength and valuation, then sized so that no single stock needs to dominate.

Index Options Selling (Nifty/Sensex) using Collateral Margins

Writing Nifty and Sensex index options to collect premium, using margin released by pledging holdings you already own. The aim is regular cash flow on top of the equity portfolio.

Risk level
High
Indicative horizon
Weekly to monthly cycles
Suited for
Investors with an existing portfolio who understand that one sharp market move can cost more than several months of collected premium.
Client-reported
20–25% yearly return from options selling
Client-reported, unaudited. Past performance is not indicative of future returns.

How it works

  1. 01

    Pledge holdings for margin

    Eligible shares or ETFs are pledged through your broker. After a haircut they count as collateral margin, while you keep ownership. Exchange rules require part of the margin to be in cash or cash equivalents.

  2. 02

    Write index options

    Nifty or Sensex options are sold at strikes chosen from volatility, open interest and key price levels. The seller receives the premium upfront and keeps it if the option expires worthless.

  3. 03

    Define the risk

    Positions can be hedged, typically by buying further out-of-the-money options. This can cap the maximum loss and also reduce the margin required.

  4. 04

    Manage to expiry

    If the market moves against a position, it can be adjusted or exited at pre-set levels instead of being held in hope.

Weekly Expiry Trading Strategies

Rule-based setups built around the weekly expiry of index options, when time decay is fastest and premiums can swing sharply within hours.

Risk level
Very high
Indicative horizon
Weekly, hours to days
Suited for
Experienced investors who commit only a small, defined slice of capital and accept that some weeks will be losing weeks.

How it works

  1. 01

    Know the calendar

    Under current exchange rules each exchange offers a weekly expiry on one benchmark index: Nifty 50 on NSE and Sensex on BSE. Strategies can be planned around those dates.

  2. 02

    Read the setup

    Implied volatility, open-interest build-up and distance to key levels decide whether a week calls for a directional trade, a neutral one, or no trade at all.

  3. 03

    Fix the risk per trade

    Each trade should have a predetermined stop-loss and position size, because near expiry an option premium can double or collapse in minutes.

  4. 04

    Close out

    Positions are typically squared off on or before expiry to avoid settlement and overnight gap risk.

IPO Research & Premium Listing Strategies

Issue-by-issue research to decide which public offers are worth applying for, with a listing-day plan in place before the shares list.

Risk level
Moderate–High
Indicative horizon
Per issue, listing day to long-term
Suited for
Investors who want selective IPO exposure based on fundamentals rather than market buzz.

How it works

  1. 01

    Read the offer document

    The red herring prospectus is reviewed for the business model, financials, use of proceeds, promoter background and the valuation asked relative to listed peers.

  2. 02

    Track subscription data

    Category-wise subscription from qualified institutional buyers, non-institutional investors and retail investors shows how the issue is being received during the bidding days.

  3. 03

    Apply sensibly

    Applications are made through ASBA or UPI. In oversubscribed issues retail allotment is decided by lottery, so an allotment is never certain.

  4. 04

    Plan listing day

    Before the shares list, a plan can be set: book gains, hold for the long term or exit at a pre-set level. Shares can also list below the issue price, and the plan should cover that case too.

Intraday & Swing Trading

Short-horizon trades driven by price action, volume and technical levels. Intraday positions close the same day; swing positions run from a few days to a few weeks.

Risk level
Very high
Indicative horizon
Intraday to a few weeks
Suited for
Active investors with surplus risk capital who accept frequent small losses as part of the method.

How it works

  1. 01

    Scan and shortlist

    Liquid stocks and indices are screened for trend, momentum, volume and clear support or resistance levels.

  2. 02

    Plan before entry

    Entry, stop-loss and target are typically fixed before the trade, with position size set so that hitting the stop costs only a small, known share of capital.

  3. 03

    Execute and trail

    Stops can be moved up to protect gains as a trade works. Losing trades are meant to be exited at the stop.

  4. 04

    Review every trade

    Logging and reviewing each trade helps the rules improve and mistakes are not repeated.

Commodity Investments in Gold & Silver ETFs

Exposure to gold and silver through exchange-traded funds held in your demat account. Precious metals often move differently from equities, which makes them a useful diversifier.

Risk level
Low–Moderate
Indicative horizon
Medium to long-term
Suited for
Most investors as a diversifier, especially those who want a hedge against inflation, rupee weakness or equity drawdowns.

How it works

  1. 01

    Set the allocation

    A portion of the portfolio can be assigned to precious metals, based on your overall goals and risk profile.

  2. 02

    Choose the ETF

    Funds are compared on liquidity, tracking error and expense ratio. Units are typically backed by metal held by the fund, so physical storage is not your concern.

  3. 03

    Build in stages

    Purchases can be staggered to average the entry price, and units trade on the exchange just like shares.

  4. 04

    Rebalance

    After a sharp rally or fall, the allocation can be brought back to target, which trims what has run up and adds to what has fallen.

Margin Trading Fund (MTF) Optimization

Using your broker's margin trading facility to buy more than your cash alone allows, only where the expected benefit clearly outweighs the interest cost and the added risk.

Risk level
High
Indicative horizon
Weeks to months
Suited for
Experienced investors who understand leverage and can add margin at short notice if prices fall.

How it works

  1. 01

    Understand the mechanics

    You pay part of the purchase value and the broker funds the rest. Only stocks on the broker's approved list qualify, and the shares bought are pledged as collateral.

  2. 02

    Count the cost

    Interest is charged daily on the funded amount for as long as the position is open, so a trade has to earn more than its financing cost to be worth it.

  3. 03

    Size conservatively

    Leverage magnifies losses as well as gains. Positions should be sized to reduce the chance that a sharp fall triggers margin calls or forced selling.

  4. 04

    Exit with discipline

    Each position should have a time limit and a stop-loss, and be closed or moved to delivery once the idea plays out or fails.

An honest note

Is F&O right for me?

SEBI studies have found that most individual F&O traders incur losses. Derivatives are leveraged and time-bound, and they punish weak risk control quickly. That is why a defined-risk approach matters: hedged positions, fixed position sizes, pre-set exits and collateral-backed margins. F&O should be considered only where it fits your risk profile, and for many investors it does not.

It may fit if you

  • Already hold a long-term portfolio that can be pledged as collateral
  • Understand that a single bad week can wipe out months of premium
  • Can follow pre-set exits without overriding them

It is probably not for you if you

  • Need the money in the near future or cannot afford losses
  • Are looking for quick or fixed income
  • Would be uncomfortable seeing a position in loss before expiry

Questions

Strategy questions, answered plainly.

Something else on your mind? Ask us directly.

What does options selling on collateral margin mean?

It means pledging shares or ETFs you already own with your broker so that they count towards the margin needed to sell options. You keep ownership of the holdings, a haircut is applied to their value, and exchange rules require part of the margin in cash or cash equivalents. The premium collected from selling Nifty or Sensex options adds cash flow, but losses on a sharp adverse move can be significant.

How is investing different from trading?

Investing means owning quality assets for years and letting earnings growth compound. Trading means taking short-term positions to capture price moves over minutes, days or weeks. Long-term investing is the core of the Easy Trading approach; trading strategies can be used selectively, within defined risk limits.

Do I need to use all eight strategies?

No. Most portfolios use only a few of them. The mix depends on your goals, time horizon and capacity for risk, and some strategies, such as weekly expiry trading or MTF, are not suitable for many investors at all.

What is the Margin Trading Facility (MTF)?

MTF lets you buy approved stocks by paying part of the purchase value while your broker funds the rest. The shares are pledged as collateral and interest is charged daily on the funded amount, so leverage increases both potential gains and potential losses. It suits only investors who understand these costs and risks.

Can any of these strategies promise fixed returns?

No. Every strategy on this page carries market risk, and returns can be lower than expected or negative. Any historical figures we share are client-reported and are not a forecast. Investments in the securities market are subject to market risks.
